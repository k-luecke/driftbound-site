Research

Research identified across the physical sciences.

Applied across the physical sciences, the pipeline has identified research that could be conducted to close a broad range of open problems — specific lines of work, each joining a method proven in one field to an unanswered question in another. The problems span fields that rarely read each other closely; the common thread is that the missing piece already exists somewhere, unrecognized as the answer.

These are proposals under development, not results. Each is framed the same way: the constraint that is really a design choice is named alongside the constraint that is a law or measurement limit, and the confirm, refute, and inconclusive rules are fixed before any data is examined. Applied Directions · the lab's own focus

Five applied problems in resilient timing, navigation, sensing, and trust — closer to the lab's active engineering capability. Each is stated as an open question with the constraint that keeps it hard.

01 · Assured time integrity

The Clock That Knows It Is Being Lied To

Can a distributed set of clocks hold verifiable time through a combined denial-and-deception attack — detecting a lying synchronization signal, not merely surviving a missing one?

Context current work largely addresses holdover — keeping time when a reference is jammed or denied. That defeats denial but not deception; a clock that holds perfectly can still be handed a manipulated re-synchronization signal.

Why it stays open with round-trip timing alone, an asymmetric path delay and a clock offset are indistinguishable. Detecting a subtle manipulation requires an independent reference that does not travel through the same channel.

02 · Navigation integrity

The Reference That Won't Hold Still

Can a magnetic-anomaly navigation system know, in real time, when its reference field is being corrupted by space weather or daily variation?

Why it stays open the stable field and the transient corruption arrive through the same sensor. They cannot be separated at a single node without an independent temporal reference.

03 · Edge trust

The Key That Survives Its Own Capture

Can a disconnected device continuously authenticate both itself and its operator, with no network callback, and degrade predictably rather than catastrophically if it is physically captured?

Why it stays open how much compromising one factor reveals about the other, once they are jointly combined, is an unresolved question in the underlying theory. This direction is held pending progress on it.

04 · Passive distributed sensing

The Net That Hears Without Listening

Can a low-cost mesh of passive sensors detect and locate a hard-to-see object from its magnetic signature — telling it apart from natural geophysical noise at useful range?

Why it stays open a magnetic signature falls off steeply with distance, a hard limit no processing can beat. It sets a floor on how dense such a mesh must be for a given range.

05 · Data provenance

The Timestamp That Outlives the Node

Can a recorded observation's time and place of origin stay verifiable after the fact, even if the device that made it is later fully compromised?

Why it stays open a device holding all of its own keys can always forge its own clock. A time that survives later compromise requires an external reference the device could not have predicted or manufactured in advance.

Active Work

A biometric-to-hash authentication pipeline

A construction that combines a biometric-derived value and a hardware-derived value before hashing, aimed at authentication that degrades gradually rather than all at once.

Status paused, pending resolution of an open theoretical question about how much compromising one source reveals about the other when the two are combined. The lab regards this open question as the honest gate on the work, and does not present the design as complete.

The framing method relates to established traditions in structured problem analysis and is used as a working tool, not claimed as original.
