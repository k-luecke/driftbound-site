Driftbound Labs

Independent research across disciplines.

Approach

Driftbound Labs is an independent research effort building a machine-learning pipeline to read across disciplines at scale. The working premise is that many difficult problems are not waiting on a new discovery so much as on a connection: a result already well established in one field can be the missing piece for an open problem in another, but the two fields rarely read each other closely. The lab's aim is to find those gaps — where a known method in one discipline answers an unanswered question in a second — and to combine existing, individually unremarkable results from each side into a solution whose novelty lies in the joining.

The pipeline is the tool for surveying the literature broadly enough to surface those pairings; the framing method below is how a candidate pairing is tested for whether the connection actually holds. This is the lab's intended way of working across a broad range of fields — a direction under development, not a demonstrated capability.

The lab uses a recurring structure to frame a problem. A candidate capability is broken into properties that have each been demonstrated somewhere in the literature but never together. The frame then names two things: a constraint the field treats as fixed that is actually a design choice (and might be engineered away), and a constraint that is a theorem, conservation law, or measurement limit (and will not move). A problem worth working on sits between the two. This structure relates to established traditions in structured problem analysis; the lab treats it as a working method, not a discovery.

Experiments are designed with the outcome rules written first: what would count as confirmation, what would count as refutation, and a defined region between them that counts as inconclusive. The aim is that a negative result is as informative as a positive one.

Open Questions

Ten problems the lab finds worth pursuing, across ten fields. Each is stated as a question, followed by the constraint that keeps it open. These are research interests, not results.

01 · Clinical neuroscience

The Bedside Witness

Is there a reading of a person's inner life that a machine can take at the bedside — needing no movement, speech, or cooperation — specific enough to trust when a family is deciding whether to withdraw care?

Why it stays open no task-based measure can distinguish absent consciousness from present consciousness that failed to engage the task. Absence of the signal is not the signal of absence — a limit of the inference, not of the instrument.

02 · Quantum gravity · working title

The First Quantum of Gravity

Can a tabletop experiment show that gravity places two masses into an entangled state — the first empirical datum on whether gravity is quantized?

Why it stays open local operations and classical communication cannot create entanglement, so entanglement mediated by gravity alone would imply a quantum mediator. But the two hardest requirements pull against each other — more mass gives more gravitational signal and more coupling to everything else.

03 · Electrocatalysis

The Cold Corner

Can a catalyst break the nitrogen triple bond at ambient temperature and pressure, with Haber–Bosch selectivity, at industrial current density, without consuming a sacrificial metal?

Why it stays open on a metal surface the binding energies of the reaction intermediates are linearly correlated, so the strength that splits nitrogen cannot be tuned independently of the weakness that releases ammonia.

04 · Origin of life · working title

The Single Pot

Can replication, metabolism, and compartmentalization coexist in one prebiotically plausible system, without a chemist steering each step?

Why it stays open replication fidelity bounds how much information a system can maintain (the error threshold), which constrains how the three functions can be assembled together.

05 · Cryopreservation · working title

The Window That Shuts

Can an organ be vitrified and rewarmed above some critical size without ice forming?

Why it stays open below a critical cooling and warming rate, ice nucleates; that rate rises with volume while cryoprotectant concentration is capped by toxicity. The two constraints close on each other as organs get larger, and where the window shuts is not known.

06 · Aging biology

The Dial Without a Cliff

Is there a dose of cellular plasticity that resets an aged cell's epigenome without loosening its grip on its own identity — delivered systemically, in a normal animal, without a tumor?

Why it stays open the same relaxation that permits rejuvenation may be the same process that permits dedifferentiation and tumor formation. If they are one variable rather than two, no dose separates them.

07 · Machine learning interpretability

The Probe That Cannot Be Trained Against

Is there a reading of a model's internal state that reports whether it believes what it is saying — and stays accurate while the model is optimized in the presence of the reading?

Why it stays open any measure that becomes a training target tends to stop being a good measure. A probe inside the optimization loop is optimized against; a probe outside it has no guarantee across distribution shift.

08 · Astrobiology

The Signature With No Twin

Is there an unambiguous biosignature — one that cannot be produced by chemistry alone?

Why it stays open there is exactly one known biosphere, so any claim about what life does versus what chemistry does is fit to a sample of one. The hardest parts are questions of inference, not of instrument sensitivity.

09 · Fusion energy

The Fuel That Makes Its Own Fuel

Can a plant breed more tritium than it burns, from a blanket that must also survive high-energy neutrons, extract the heat, and leave room for the magnets and the ports?

Why it stays open one deuterium–tritium fusion yields exactly one neutron, and more than one tritium must return from it. Every structural atom, port, and parasitic capture eats a margin set by nuclear physics.

10 · Antibiotics · working title

The Gate With Two Locks

Is there a rule that predicts whether a molecule will accumulate inside a Gram-negative bacterium — across species, for both entry and removal?

Why it stays open accumulation is entry minus efflux, and the two terms select on partly opposing properties, so any rule built on a single descriptor is structurally under-determined.

Applied Directions

Five applied problems in resilient timing, navigation, sensing, and trust. These are closer to the lab's active engineering capability. Same convention: stated as open questions with the constraint that keeps each one hard.

01 · Assured time integrity

The Clock That Knows It Is Being Lied To

Can a distributed set of clocks hold verifiable time through a combined denial-and-deception attack — detecting a lying synchronization signal, not merely surviving a missing one — from a reference an adversary cannot manipulate in transit?

Context current work in the field largely addresses holdover — keeping accurate time when a reference is jammed or denied. That defeats denial but not deception; a clock that holds perfectly can still be handed a manipulated re-synchronization signal.

Why it stays open with round-trip timing measurements alone, an asymmetric path delay and a clock offset are indistinguishable. Detecting a subtle manipulation therefore requires an independent reference that does not travel through the same channel.

02 · Navigation integrity

The Reference That Won't Hold Still

Can a magnetic-anomaly navigation system know, in real time, when its reference field is being corrupted by space weather or daily variation — the transient effects that currently force pre-surveyed maps and in-mission calibration?

Why it stays open the stable field and the transient corruption arrive through the same sensor. They cannot be separated at a single node without an independent temporal reference.

03 · Edge trust

The Key That Survives Its Own Capture

Can a disconnected device continuously authenticate both itself and its operator, with no network callback, and degrade predictably rather than catastrophically if it is physically captured?

Why it stays open how much compromising one factor (the device or the operator) reveals about the other, once they are jointly combined, is an unresolved question in the underlying theory. This direction is held pending progress on that question.

04 · Passive distributed sensing

The Net That Hears Without Listening

Can a low-cost mesh of passive sensors detect and locate a hard-to-see object from its magnetic signature — telling it apart from natural geophysical noise at useful range?

Why it stays open a magnetic signature falls off steeply with distance, a hard limit no processing can beat. It sets a floor on how dense such a mesh must be for a given range.

05 · Data provenance

The Timestamp That Outlives the Node

Can a recorded observation's time and place of origin stay verifiable after the fact, even if the device that made it is later fully compromised?

Why it stays open a device holding all of its own keys can always forge its own clock. Establishing a time that survives later compromise requires an external reference the device could not have predicted or manufactured in advance.

Active Work

One current project, described in honest scope.

A biometric-to-hash authentication pipeline

A construction that combines a biometric-derived value and a hardware-derived value before hashing, aimed at authentication that degrades gradually rather than all at once.

Status paused, pending resolution of an open theoretical question about how much compromising one source reveals about the other when the two are combined. The lab regards this open question as the honest gate on the work, and does not present the design as complete.
