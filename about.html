The lab

Driftbound Labs is an independent effort that works across a broad range of fields. Its premise is that difficult problems often turn on a connection between disciplines rather than a single new discovery — a result well established in one field that answers an open question in another. The lab looks for those pairings and tries to join existing results into something new at the seam.

A few standards hold regardless of subject: work is pre-registered where possible, with confirm, refute, and inconclusive rules fixed before data is examined; artifacts are hashed so results can be checked against what was actually run; and precision and noise limits are stated rather than omitted. A negative result is treated as a result. The framing method the lab uses relates to established traditions in structured problem analysis and is treated as a working tool, not an invention.

About the founder

Kyle Luecke is the founder of Driftbound Labs.

His work emphasizes pre-registration, fixed decision rules, and hashed artifacts, so results can be checked against what was actually run. He has completed a pre-registered two-station geophysical time-series analysis end to end, with null tests declared before the first run; built cryptographic verification in Rust, with aggregate signatures checked against live production data; and, out of personal curiosity, taken hardware description language from first contact to working finite-field designs.

His day job is delivering physical network infrastructure at scale, including fiber outside plant and PON/OLT systems — the environments where timing and sensing equipment actually get installed. Earlier, he worked in a cognition laboratory under Dr. Sharron Mutter.

The lab's direction follows from that practice: treat the scientific method as operational, keep claims bounded by measurement, and build toward a data pipeline that can identify tractable frontier problems in the physical sciences.
