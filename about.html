The lab

Driftbound Labs is a small independent effort that works across a broad range of fields. Its premise is that difficult problems often turn on a connection rather than a single new discovery — a result well established in one place that answers an open question in another. The gap can be between fields, or between specialties within a single field; the lab looks for those pairings and joins existing results into something new at the seam.

Method

The lab's method merges three established practices to surface research directions that are at once genuinely novel and reasonable to pursue, then adds a requirement of its own.

Literature-based discovery (after Swanson) finds connections between fields — or between specialties within one field — that do not cite each other. This is where a pairing nobody has made comes from.

Morphological analysis (after Zwicky and Ritchey) maps which combinations of known results have never been tried together, turning a loose connection into a structured, checkable candidate.

Strong inference (after Platt) admits only questions a decisive test can settle, filtering out the merely clever.

Novelty comes from the first, a tractable test from the third, and the second connects them. To these the lab adds a requirement of its own: no candidate is pursued until it is anchored to a fundamental limit — a hard floor traced to a theorem, conservation law, or measurement bound. The three practices are established and credited as such; the assembly and that floor are the lab's own.

Work is run to a few standards regardless of subject: pre-registered where possible, with confirm, refute, and inconclusive rules fixed before data is examined; artifacts hashed so results can be checked against what was actually run; and precision and noise limits stated rather than omitted. A negative result is treated as a result.

About the founder

Kyle Luecke is the founder of Driftbound Labs.

His work emphasizes pre-registration, fixed decision rules, and hashed artifacts, so results can be checked against what was actually run. He has completed a pre-registered two-station geophysical time-series analysis end to end, with null tests declared before the first run; built cryptographic verification in Rust, with aggregate signatures checked against live production data; and, out of personal curiosity, implemented finite-field arithmetic in a hardware description language, verified bit-exact against a software reference.

His day job is delivering physical network infrastructure at scale, including fiber outside plant and PON/OLT systems — the environments where timing and sensing equipment actually get installed. Earlier, he worked in a cognition laboratory under Dr. Sharron Mutter.

Driftbound Labs is his own initiative. Its direction: treat the scientific method as operational, keep claims bounded by measurement, and build toward a data pipeline that can identify tractable frontier problems in the physical sciences.
