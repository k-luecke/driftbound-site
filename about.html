Driftbound Labs LLC

Driftbound Labs (Driftbound Labs LLC) is an applied research and engineering company founded by Kyle Luecke. It serves both as the operating company behind commercial verification infrastructure, physical-layer capital-project intelligence, and scoped technical advisory, and as an independent laboratory conducting preregistered frontier research with auditable, hash-locked artifacts.

The commercial thesis is straightforward: where capital allocation, autonomous software, or critical infrastructure depends on an unverified assumption, build the smallest rigorous instrument that makes the underlying physical or cryptographic state checkable. The long-horizon research goal is to automate eureka.

Public surfaces across the organization: Ventures & Advisory (commercial platforms, API licensing, and diligence), paxiom.org (proof-of-delivery receipts & Base Mainnet verifier operated by Driftbound Labs LLC), Papers & Standards (preprints, SHA-256 digests, and OpenTimestamps proofs), termbrow.org (Kyle's technical engineering notes, open-source terminal reading browser, and 20-project archive), GitHub, and ORCID 0009-0008-0202-7852.

About the founder

Kyle Luecke's passion for science came early. Childhood visits to Fermilab, and afternoons walking the aisles of American Science & Surplus in Geneva, Illinois, are among his fondest memories. As he got older he put that curiosity to work with his hands, experimenting with composite-material construction — bike and skateboard parts, and even shoe soles, with varying degrees of success.

In college, despite STEM-rooted interests, he studied psychology with a focus on cognition and biobehavioral dynamics — working for a time in a cognition laboratory under Dr. Sharron Mutter — intending to continue to an MS and later a PhD. Learning about learning served him well, but reality outside school intervened before those plans were finished.

He worked as a glazier, then an electrician, and eventually as an assistant project manager on a very large outside-plant fiber buildout in the Nashville metro area — the kind of physical network infrastructure (fiber outside plant, PON/OLT systems) where timing and sensing equipment actually get installed.

The curiosity never stopped. Off the clock it took the shape of a self-study practice sustained over several years: John Ehlers' blend of signal processing and financial markets, finite-field arithmetic and zero-knowledge proofs, and using publicly available data to do real scientific work at home, for its own sake. Whether reading about zero-knowledge-proof acceleration or trying to contribute to CrunchDAO's structural-break research, he kept at it.

That practice set the company's habits — pre-registration, fixed decision rules, and hashed artifacts, so results can be checked against what was actually run. He has completed a pre-registered two-station geophysical time-series analysis end to end, with null tests declared before the first run; shipped cryptographic receipt verification across TypeScript, Python, MCP, and Base Mainnet; built an experimental x86-64 Rust capability microkernel with an OCaml 5 control plane; and implemented finite-field arithmetic in SystemVerilog verified bit-exact against a software reference.

Artificial intelligence, and programs like DARPA's ERIS that solicit work from independent technical organizations, have made it possible to pair commercial software spinouts with primary research — building durable leverage from verifiable results rather than credentials alone.
