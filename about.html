The lab

Driftbound Labs is a small independent effort that works across a broad range of fields. Its premise is that difficult problems often turn on a connection rather than a single new discovery — a result well established in one place that answers an open question in another. The gap can be between fields, or between specialties within a single field; the lab looks for those pairings and joins existing results into something new at the seam.

The long-horizon goal is to automate eureka — to make the leap of connection a repeatable process rather than a lucky accident.

About the founder

Kyle Luecke is the founder of Driftbound Labs.

A passion for science came early. Student days at Fermilab and afternoons walking the aisles of American Science & Surplus in Geneva, Illinois are among his fondest childhood memories. As he got older he put that curiosity to work with his hands, experimenting with composite-material construction — bike and skateboard parts, and even shoe soles, with varying degrees of success.

In college, despite STEM-rooted interests, he studied psychology with a focus on cognition and bio-behaviorism — working for a time in a cognition laboratory under Dr. Sharron Mutter — intending to continue to an MS and later a PhD. Learning about learning served him well, but reality outside school intervened before those plans were finished.

He worked as a glazier, then an electrician, and eventually as an assistant project manager on a very large outside-plant fiber buildout for GFiber in the Nashville metro area — the kind of physical network infrastructure (fiber outside plant, PON/OLT systems) where timing and sensing equipment actually get installed.

His passion for science and learning never stopped. Off the clock it took the shape of a self-study practice sustained over several years: John Ehlers' blend of signal processing and financial markets, finite-field arithmetic and zero-knowledge proofs, and using publicly available data to do real scientific work at home, for its own sake. Whether reading about zero-knowledge-proof acceleration or trying to contribute to CrunchDAO's structural-break research, he kept at it.

That practice set the lab's habits — pre-registration, fixed decision rules, and hashed artifacts, so results can be checked against what was actually run. He has completed a pre-registered two-station geophysical time-series analysis end to end, with null tests declared before the first run; built cryptographic verification in Rust, with aggregate signatures checked against live production data; and, out of personal curiosity, implemented finite-field arithmetic in a hardware description language, verified bit-exact against a software reference.

Artificial intelligence and resources like DARPA's ERIS program have made it possible to go deeper — to pursue a career in research by producing results rather than chasing credentials, at least for now. Driftbound Labs is his own initiative: treat the scientific method as operational, keep claims bounded by measurement, and build toward a data pipeline that can identify tractable frontier problems in the physical sciences.
