Scope & failed stability gate — Preregistered gate D2b is not supported: while 11/12 thirty-minute windows were individually significant (p ≤ 0.05) with a physically bounded median lag (−3.85 ms), between-window scatter (MADN = 4.95 ms) exceeded the 0.796 ms stationarity threshold by 6.2× (proposed mechanism: diurnal lightning-source migration; companion WWLLN tests in k-luecke/Geo also not supported). 02 · Agentic Systems · Runtime Verification · Security

Typed Boundaries for Small-Model Web Agents

Kyle W. Luecke · Driftbound Labs · v1.0 (September 2026) · 21 pages

5/5 Bitcoin OpenTimestamps (.ots) Confirmed Zero Invalid Actions Executed

Evaluates a non-learned typed runtime boundary (SCP)—combining a compact accessibility-tree projection with a deterministic four-rung guard ladder (D1–D4) and provenance-preserving abstention—on MiniWoB++ under BrowserGym (25 tasks × 10 seeds) and a confirmatory 300-world synthetic cohort. Across Qwen2.5 0.5B, 1.5B, and 3B models, SCP tracks the reference agent on task success (+0.8, +0.4, +0.8 pp) at roughly one-third of exact BPE observation tokens (3.1×–3.8× reduction) with zero invalid actions executed (0/300 committed violations at D4 vs. 152/300 unassisted, and 0 vs. 88 violations against an FP16 teacher-forced confidence baseline).

Mechanism & boundaries — The gate filters and does not repair: synthetic cohort task success was identically 101/300 across every rung; ±10 pp TOST equivalence was supported at 0.5B ([−2.4, +4.0] pp), marginal at 1.5B, and unbounded at 3B; preregistered superiority over raw accessibility trees was not supported (+6.0 pp, p = 0.24 vs. α = 0.01). On Qwen3.5-2B, an attribution ladder shows action-schema typing (+22.4 pp) accounts for completion lift rather than observation compression alone.

Phase Selects; It Does Not Amplify: A Preregistered Test of Oscillatory Control as Expert Selection

Kyle W. Luecke · Driftbound Labs · v1.0 (September 2026) · 13 pages

12/12 Bitcoin OpenTimestamps (.ots) Confirmed Stages T5–T13 Preregistered

Tests across twelve preregistered experimental stages whether an oscillatory division of labor—slow tonic selection stencils paired with phase-tagged expert superposition—does useful work inside a small Mixture-of-Experts transformer. Fixed-dynamics coupled units hold an expert-selection regime after control input stops (Thold = 26.8 tokens at n = 8; 65.0 tokens at n = 3 with ρeff = 0.989 vs. <2 tokens stateless), obey cosine lock-in demodulation (X(Δφ) ≈ max(0, cos Δφ)), and act as a single-pass expert-attribution medium in looped MoE under matched compute (rφ = 0.869).

Falsifications & process record — Coherent superposition produced no ensemble amplification (Gmedium = 0.044 vs. Gsum = 0.251); under continuous evidence, stateless lookup matched or outperformed persistent coordination; across mid-gap lures (T12b), held state beat one-step memory (0.6525 vs. 0.2987) but tied k = 3 majority smoothing (0.6390); and stage T10 was closed as not informatively tested with its classifier claim withdrawn due to a disclosed tag-placement side-channel.

Network-Native Computation: Foundations, Structural Leverage, External Validity, and the Limits of Prospective Governance

Kyle W. Luecke · Driftbound Labs · v1.0 (October 2026) · 14 pages

8 Reproducible Micro-Experiments Theorem 1 Lower Bound + Exp08 Negative Result

Investigates when ordinary, RFC-compliant protocol transitions and continuously maintained substrate invariants evaluate computational distinctions at lower physical cost than local execution without deploying custom code into network nodes. Across eight experiments: DNS DNAME/CNAME rewriting evaluates finite automata with 100% agreement but runs 153.6×–268.6× slower than local CPU (Exp01); Merkle frontier reconciliation achieves 25.2× wall speedup and 3,158× wire reduction at N = 218, k = 1 while explicit mutation logs (Δ) beat NNC by 8.9× when available (Exp02–Exp04); and geometry-dependent frontier scaling survives stock Git v2.55.0 (23.8× wire cut; |F| = 19 clustered vs. 34 scattered at k = 16), 6-peer IPFS Bitswap + Kademlia DHT swarms (13.9×–48.5× wire cut), and AO/Arweave checkpoint-witness emulation (Exp05–Exp07).

Prospective governance boundary — In Experiment 08 (200 stratified held-out workloads, probe budget B ≤ 8), zero-hindsight controllers failed pre-specified thresholds (ηt = 1.803 > 1.25; catastrophic misroute rate 11.0% > 5.0%) and lost to unconditional bulk streaming (ηt = 1.361), explained by Theorem 1, the Merkle Ambiguity Lemma, and tail-loss asymmetry (27.4× vs. 3.4×).

05 · Infrastructure Research · Power Grids · Survival Analysis

The Physical Layer of the AI Infrastructure Buildout

Kyle W. Luecke · Driftbound Labs · Institutional Research Whitepaper (September 2026) · 9 pages

Hash-Locked Cohort Manifests 31 Builds · 2,184 MW Audited

Reconstructs point-in-time utility interconnection dockets, municipal structural permits, and statutory 811 excavation telemetry across 31 evaluable North American hyperscale data centers and commercial semiconductor fabs (2,184 MW announced load). Physical or regulatory divergence preceded corporate schedule revision in 9 of 9 resolved delay cases with an arithmetic median lead of 211 calendar days (IQR 200–225 days); >90-day slip scaled monotonically across four utility interconnection states (0.0%, 37.5%, 100.0%, 100.0%); only 1,128 MW (51.6%) cleared a three-condition execution gate; right-censored Kaplan–Meier substation energization reached a 112.0-week step median across 18 transitions; and point-in-time physical features reduced Brier calibration error by 84.5% (0.0383 vs. 0.2476) across 8 of 8 held-out test builds.

Evidentiary boundary — Cell sizes in the most constrained queue tiers (N = 5 and N = 4) and the held-out replay partition (N = 8) are modest, and the Brier evaluation is a point-in-time retrospective replay with zero live prospective resolutions claimed in this release.

Paxiom Receipts (paxiom.receipt.v0) & x402 Delivery-Receipt Extension

Open Specification, Public Reference Verifier & Zero-Dependency SDKs · v0.2.0 (2026)

JCS / Raw SHA-256 + Ed25519 npm · PyPI · MCP Registry

An open, vendor-neutral cryptographic receipt format (Payment-Receipt) binding HTTP requests, canonical JSON (jcs-sha256) or binary (raw-sha256) outputs, seller key discovery (/.well-known/x402-keys.json), and CAIP-2 / x402 payment references under Ed25519 signatures. Offline checks verify integrity without vendor callbacks, reject unauthorized signer substitution as unauthorized_issuer, and label unchecked settlement claims explicitly as claimed_not_checked.
